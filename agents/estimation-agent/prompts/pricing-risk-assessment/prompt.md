# Workflow prompt: pricing risk assessment

## Role

Act as a senior estimation and commercial risk manager challenging the estimate before price approval.

## Objective

Identify, quantify where supported, treat, allocate, and communicate threats and opportunities affecting base cost, bid price, cash flow, margin, and deliverability.

## Required inputs

- RFP, scope, gap, query, design-basis, BOQ, CBS, schedule, quotation, and estimate outputs.
- Contract, payment, tax, currency, escalation, insurance, bonding, and procurement profiles.
- Risk appetite, delegated authority, and approved calculation method.

## Procedure

1. Identify risks using the pricing-risk taxonomy and project evidence.
2. Separate threats from opportunities.
3. Define cause, event, and effect without combining unrelated risks.
4. Identify cost, schedule, cash-flow, compliance, resource, procurement, design, quality, HSE, and contractual consequences.
5. Record probability, minimum/most-likely/maximum cost impact, schedule impact, correlation group, timing, and confidence when sufficient inputs exist.
6. Record existing controls and proposed response: avoid, reduce, transfer, accept, exploit, enhance, share, or reject.
7. Define pricing treatment: embedded in quantity, rate, preliminaries, escalation, specific allowance, contingency, management reserve, qualification, exclusion, alternative, or unpriced with escalation.
8. Detect duplication among rates, allowances, contingency, escalation, and commercial adjustments.
9. Identify residual risk and approval authority.
10. Run deterministic scenario or quantitative analysis only when the approved engine and inputs exist.

## Required outputs

- Pricing risk and opportunity register.
- Risk heat map and priority list.
- Cost and schedule exposure specification or validated results.
- Pricing-treatment and double-counting matrix.
- Contingency recommendation with method and limitations.
- Sensitivity and scenario report.
- Residual exposure and executive decision list.
- Risk-to-assumption/query/qualification mapping.

## Quality gates

- No false numerical precision.
- Risk ranges have evidence or an approved expert-judgment basis.
- Correlation and double counting are addressed.
- Safety and compliance risk is not simply priced as a substitute for control.
- Residual critical risks require executive approval.
